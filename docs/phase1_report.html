What was achieved

  • A working two-stage system — 88.5M trained parameters across a BiLSTM masking stage and a 9-block DiT flow-matching stage, plus a HiFi-GAN vocoder trained from scratch on this project's own mel configuration.
  • Three characterized limitations diagnosed, two substantially mitigated. The catastrophic-outlier rate fell 3.6× (12.7% → 3.5%); the sub-1 dB SNR coverage gap was closed (low-SNR accuracy 61.3% → 80.3%); speaker-count generalization improved from 72.6% to 77.0% at four speakers.
  • Cross-corpus validation on Libri2Mix, a benchmark built independently of this project's mixer, confirming the gains are not an artifact of the in-house data pipeline.
  • A quantified ceiling (§8): the remaining extraction headroom is ~4.6 pp and lies almost entirely in Stage 1, while the measurement chain itself costs ~9.7 pp.

Method note

From the low-SNR campaign onward, every candidate was judged against a decision rule written down before its results were seen — a target, a set of retention bars, and what to do in each outcome. Fifteen candidate changes were evaluated in Phase One; four were promoted and eleven rejected, several of which looked like improvements on the metric that motivated them.

One promotion (§7, entry K) knowingly overrode two of its own pre-registered bars. That override, and the evidence for it, is reported here rather than smoothed away.

02Problem and objective

Target speaker extraction (TSE) isolates one chosen talker from a mixture of simultaneous voices, given a short reference recording of that talker. Unlike blind source separation, which returns every source and leaves the caller to decide which is which, TSE returns exactly one signal and must decide identity itself. That makes speaker identity — not just signal quality — the thing that has to be right.

Objective

Reproduce Moon et al., Mask2Flow-TSE (arXiv:2603.12837, submitted to Interspeech 2026), from the paper text alone. No public implementation exists, so every architectural and training decision had to be derived from the description and then verified against measured behaviour.

What counts as success

Three things, in order of weight:

  1. Signal quality — SI-SDR improvement over the unprocessed mixture, the standard metric in the separation literature and therefore the one comparable to outside work.
  2. Speaker fidelity — verification accuracy of the extracted audio against the enrollment clip. This is the metric specific to target extraction: a system can score acceptably on SI-SDR while leaking the wrong talker.
  3. Honesty of the record — limitations characterized with a root cause rather than reported as aggregate scores that hide them. Several results below are negative, and they are the more useful half of the report.

Signal path

  1. Mixture waveform → log-mel spectrogram (80 mels, n_fft 1024, hop 160, win 400 at 16 kHz — a Whisper-aligned configuration).
  2. Enrollment waveform → WavLM → projection → 512-d speaker vector d. WavLM consumes the raw waveform directly; it never touches this project's mel pipeline.
  3. Stage 1: X_enh = X ⊙ M, mask in [0,1] from a sigmoid, applied to the log-mel.
  4. Stage 2: four Euler steps of a rectified-flow velocity field, conditioned on d, with classifier-free guidance at scale 2.5.
  5. HiFi-GAN vocoder → waveform.

Stage 1 — masking

Four Conv2d layers into a 2-layer BiLSTM (416 hidden units), matching the paper's §5.2.2. The mask multiplies the log-mel, exactly as the paper's Eq. 9 specifies. The speaker vector is concatenated at both LSTM layers, not only the input.

Figure 2  Stage 1. The final step is the one that matters for §7: the mask is applied to the original log-mel, whose quiet bins are negative — which is why "multiply by a value in [0,1]" does not mean "attenuate".

A structural finding about this formulation. This project's log-mel is log(mel + 1e-8), so every bin quieter than unit linear power is negative. Multiplying a negative value by a mask in [0,1] moves it toward zero — that is, makes it louder. So "pure deletion" holds only where the log-mel is non-negative. Measured on real audio: 74.2% of mixture bins are negative at low SNR, and 73.5% of target bins are unreachable by any mask in [0,1].

The paper reports D=100% / I=0% (deletion only, no insertion) and does not specify its log offset or base, so this is an under-specified paper detail rather than an implementation error. It became the central question of the low-SNR campaign (§7).

Stage 2 — flow matching

A 9-block DiT (768-dim, 8 heads) with rotary position embeddings and AdaLN-Zero conditioning. Trained with a rectified-flow objective on frozen Stage 1 output, so Stage 2 only ever learns to correct the specific error distribution Stage 1 produces — a coupling that explains several later results (§7 entries J, N and §9).

Inference integrates the velocity field with four Euler steps. Classifier-free guidance blends conditional and unconditional velocities: v = v_uncond + s·(v_cond − v_uncond).

Architecture verification

The architecture diagrams supplied at the start of the project were audited line-by-line against the running code, and four real errors were found and corrected: the reference-audio path was drawn through the mel extractor (WavLM takes raw waveform); inference was drawn as a single Euler step (the deployed configuration runs four); a final AdaLN modulation layer was missing entirely — confirmed by an exact parameter-count match against the training log's Output: 1,242,704 params; and the vocoder's "element-wise sum" is in fact an average. The corrected diagrams were rebuilt as Mermaid so they live in the repository as checked-in text and cannot silently drift from the code again.

04Training method

Data and mixture construction

Mixtures are built on the fly rather than pre-generated, so every epoch sees fresh speaker pairings and SNRs. Each sample draws a target speaker, one or more interferers, and a reference utterance that is always a different recording of the target speaker.

Curriculum fine-tuning

Rather than retraining from scratch for each hypothesis, most changes were made as fine-tunes from a known checkpoint with a modified sampling curriculum. This makes a change attributable: only the curriculum differs, so a difference in results is a difference in that curriculum.

Two curricula were used repeatedly: a speaker-count curriculum drawing 2, 3 or 4 total speakers at P = 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2, and a low-SNR curriculum drawing a fraction of samples from [−10, 1) dB. A design rule emerged and was applied consistently: when fine-tuning for a new condition, keep the curriculum that produced the validated behaviour switched on, or the fine-tune hands back the previous win while chasing the new one.

The validation loss was never trusted as a selection signal. The validation set is 2-speaker at trained SNR, so a fine-tune training on a harder mixture is measured on an easier distribution than it is learning. Across five fine-tunes the proxy was flat or worse while the real metrics improved — and once (the Stage 2 adaptation, §7 K) it improved while two headline metrics worsened. Checkpoint selection therefore used the final step, not the best-by-validation step, and promotion always required the full battery.

05Evaluation method

Three metric families, deliberately given different weights, because they disagree — and the disagreements turned out to be informative rather than noise.

Metric hierarchy and rationale
TierMetricWhy this weight
PrimarySI-SDR improvementField standard; comparable to outside work. Reported as a median, because the mean is dominated by the outlier tail.
SecondarySpeaker-verification accuracy (1 − EER), AUCThe metric specific to target extraction. SI-SDR alone cannot tell whether the right talker was extracted.
DiagnosticMel-MSE improvement, catastrophic rateInternal only. "% improvement in mel MSE" is unbounded and non-standard, so it is never a headline — but it is what located every failure mode in §9.

The evaluation battery

Every promotion candidate ran the same five-stage battery, ordered so the decisive stage runs first and a bad candidate can be cancelled early:

  1. Controlled in-domain low-SNR test, n=300 at [−10,1) dB — same corpus, mixer, speakers and seed as the baseline, so only the checkpoint differs.
  2. Libri2Mix cross-corpus, n=6000 — yields both the in-distribution and out-of-distribution slices from one run.
  3. In-domain full test-clean, n=2620 — the primary headline.
  4. Corpus-wide verification accuracy, n=400.
  5. Speaker-count conditions: 2, 3 and 4 speakers, n=300 each.

Paired-comparison discipline. Each battery reports two checkpoint-independent probes — the unprocessed mixture and the ground-truth target — passed through the identical chain. If those match the baseline run to four decimals, the two runs scored the same samples and the comparison is genuinely paired.

This check earned its keep twice. When enrollment length and the speaker projection were changed (§7 O, Q), the probes moved — revealing that those changes had altered the reference embedding itself, so the apparent system numbers were being measured on a different scale rather than reflecting better extraction.

Cross-corpus validation

Libri2Mix is generated by an independent toolchain with no SNR floor, so 60.6% of its mix_clean samples place the target below the interferer — a regime this project's mixer structurally cannot produce. Splitting on that boundary separates a generalization question from a coverage question.

Libri2Mix, n=6000, split at the trained SNR floor
SliceShareMedian mel S2 vs S1CatastrophicSI-SDR vs S1
In-distribution (≥1 dB)39.4%73.4%5.7%+2.37 dB
Out-of-distribution (<1 dB)60.6%−19.0%40.3%−1.50 dB

The in-distribution slice (73.4% / 5.7%) is consistent with — and on SI-SDR better than — the in-house headline of 77.7% / 3.5%, on a corpus this project did not build. That is the evidence that the gains are real and not a property of the in-house mixer. The out-of-distribution slice is what motivated the low-SNR campaign, and was subsequently improved to +11.2% mel and 28.2% catastrophic.

06bProcesses run

Every repeatable procedure in the project — what it does, why it exists, and what it produced. These are the building blocks the decision log in §7 is assembled from.

Training procedures

Stage 1 training

training/train_mask.py — 200k steps, ~47 h

Why
Produce the masking network that gives Stage 2 a cleaner starting point than the raw mixture. Runs first because Stage 2 trains against its frozen output.
Result
Converged at step 130,000; that checkpoint served the whole project until the low-SNR fine-tune replaced it on 2026-09-12 — the first Stage 1 change in six weeks.

Stage 2 training

training/train_flow.py — 300k steps

Why
Learn the rectified-flow velocity field that carries Stage 1's output toward the clean target, conditioned on the speaker vector.
Result
The base checkpoint behind every later fine-tune. Trained on frozen Stage 1 output, which is what makes the two stages co-adapted — the property behind the §7 N and §9.4 findings.

Vocoder training

vocoder_train/ — HiFi-GAN from scratch, 170k steps

Why
This project's mel configuration (80 mels, hop 160, Whisper-aligned) is not compatible with any standard pre-trained vocoder, so one had to be trained on the project's own representation.
Result
Frozen since 2026-08-20 and used for every audio-domain measurement since. Also, as §8 shows, one of the two components that bound the 90.3% ceiling.

Speaker projection training

30k steps — 393,728 trainable parameters on a frozen WavLM

Why
Map WavLM's output into the 512-d conditioning space both stages consume. Keeping WavLM frozen holds the parameter cost to a third of a million.
Result
Serves both roles — extraction conditioning and the reference embedding for the accuracy metric. §7 Q shows why that dual role makes the metric sensitive to changes here.

Curriculum fine-tuning

25k steps from a promoted checkpoint, ~13 h

Why
Test a hypothesis about training coverage without a full retrain. Only the sampling curriculum changes, so any measured difference is attributable to it.
Result
Six fine-tunes in Phase One: hard-t0, hard-multispeaker, 50% and 25% low-SNR, Stage-1 low-SNR, Stage-2 adaptation, log-gain pair, onset. Three promoted, five rejected.

Evaluation procedures

The promotion battery

scripts/run_eval_candidate_gpu.sh <ckpt> <tag> [mask] [cfg] [pad] [warmup] — ~2 h

Why
One generic evaluator for any candidate, so each experiment does not need a near-duplicate script and every candidate is judged on identical ground. Outputs are tagged, so results never collide with the canonical files the docs cite.
Result
Ran for all fifteen candidates. Stage ordering puts the decisive test first so a bad candidate can be cancelled without spending the full two hours.

Cheap pre-screen

scripts/run_diag_gate_candidate_gpu.sh — ~25 min

Why
The onset gate is invisible to every battery metric, so a candidate can pass the full battery and still sound wrong. This measures only the gate.
Result
Rejected the onset fine-tune for 25 minutes of GPU instead of two hours.

Libri2Mix generation and evaluation

scripts/generate_libri2mix_test.sh then eval/eval_libri2mix.py — n=6000

Why
Every in-house number comes from one mixer. An independently built corpus tests whether the results are properties of the system or of the data pipeline.
Result
Confirmed the hard-t0 fix generalizes, and surfaced the sub-1 dB coverage gap that drove the whole low-SNR campaign. Also the first real exercise of the variable-length chunked inference path, since Libri2Mix mixtures are not a fixed 10 s.

Oracle Stage 1 substitution

eval/eval_multi_speaker.py --stage1_mode oracle_logmask | oracle_energy

Why
Answer "is Stage 1 the bottleneck?" without training anything. Two oracles separate a learnable shortfall from a formulation limit, which have opposite remedies.
Result
Figure 5. Established that both gaps are real, both open at low SNR, and — critically — that the system is near-saturated at trained SNR. This is the measurement that redirected Phase Two.

Onset diagnostics

eval/diag_onset_padding.py — padding, splice, cfg and checkpoint sweeps

Why
A defect reported by ear needed to be made measurable before it could be attacked. The measure is output level versus ground truth over the first 0.5 s of speech.
Result
Localized it to sequence position 0 (interior onsets are clean at +0.29 dB), ruled out the operating point, and then the 2×2 checkpoint ablation proved it is an interaction between the two promotions.

Listening export

eval/export_listening_samples.py — playable wav across 2/3/4 speakers

Why
No aggregate metric guarantees the output sounds like the target speaker. A human check is the only test of that.
Result
Ran twice. The first pass confirmed extraction sounds correct and found a trailing-silence bug that had been understating the numbers. The second pass found the onset gate — a defect no metric in the battery could see.

Operational procedures

Checkpoint promotion

back up the outgoing file, copy in, verify both by MD5

Why
There is no central config field for checkpoint paths — thirteen scripts name flow_best.pt as an argparse default. "Promoting" therefore means physically replacing a file, which is irreversible without a backup.
Result
Four promotions, each with a verified backup retained. Every superseded checkpoint is still recoverable.

Pre-registered decision rules

written into the project record before results are seen

Why
With this many candidates and this many metrics, there is always some cut on which a change looks good. Fixing the target and the retention bars in advance removes that freedom.
Result
Applied to every candidate from the low-SNR campaign onward. Eleven rejections, of which several would plausibly have been talked into a promotion after the fact. One deliberate override, reported in §7 K.

07Decision log

Every change evaluated in Phase One, in order, with the reason it was attempted and the reason for its verdict. Eleven of fifteen were rejected; the rejections carry most of the information.

1055508-22

Safety-net fallback Rejected

A per-frame energy-collapse detector that fell back to Stage 1 output when Stage 2 appeared to fail, intended to remove catastrophic outliers.

It removed them — and collapsed the median from 75.8% to 46.6%, because it triggered on roughly 16% of all frames, not just the failing ones. It also did nothing for speaker similarity.

Reason for rejectionA fix that fires far outside the failure it targets is not a fix; it trades a small tail for a large median.
1060608-28

Hard-t0 fine-tune Promoted

Diagnosis first: for the catastrophic samples, ‖v_cond − v_uncond‖ at t=0 ran 5–40× its magnitude at any later step. At t=0 the input equals Stage 1's output exactly, so the velocity field must extrapolate the whole trajectory from one frozen point, and guidance amplifies that disagreement into an oversized first Euler step the remaining steps cannot correct.

Fine-tuning with over-weighted t≈0 sampling cut the catastrophic rate 12.7% → 3.5%, improved median mel 67.3% → 77.7%, and SI-SDR +0.93 → +1.58 dB — improving in every SNR bucket.

Reason for promotionThe improvement appeared where the diagnosis predicted it, not merely in an aggregate insensitive to outliers.
1087509-03

Hard-multispeaker fine-tune Promoted

The model had only ever trained on 2-speaker mixtures; 3- and 4-speaker were entirely out of distribution, scoring 76.7% and 72.6%.

A 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2 speaker-count curriculum lifted these to 81.9% and 76.7% while 2-speaker held flat — gains concentrated exactly where coverage had been missing.

Reason for promotionLarge gains in the targeted condition, no regression in the validated one.

50% low-SNR curriculum, Stage 2 Rejected

Target regime won decisively: low-SNR accuracy 61.3% → 71.3%, SI-SDR −5.94 → +4.11 dB.

But corpus-wide accuracy fell 86.5% → 82.0% and the in-domain catastrophic rate rose 3.7% → 15.5% — a 4.2× increase. Per-bucket analysis showed genuine capacity reallocation, not noise.

Reason for rejectionThe cost was not confined to the diagnostic metric, unlike the two prior fine-tunes.

Reason for promotionAll four pre-registered bars passed. The decline was budgeted in advance, not discovered afterwards. First Stage 1 promotion since 2026-08-02.
1110609-13

Stage 2 adaptation to the new Stage 1 Promoted with override

After the Stage 1 promotion, Stage 2 was subtracting at low SNR (−1.40 dB vs Stage 1) because its input distribution had moved. Re-adapting it met the target decisively: S2-vs-S1 −1.40 → +1.79 dB, low-SNR accuracy 69.7% → 78.7%.

But two retention bars failed: in-domain mel catastrophic 4.6% → 9.7% (bar ≤5%) and Libri2Mix in-distribution 4.9% → 7.3% (bar ≤6%).

Reason for the overrideBoth failing bars were the mel proxy, and the SI-SDR severity check contradicted them: severe audio regressions barely moved (2.1% → 2.7%), while the share of mel-"catastrophic" cases whose audio was actually fine rose to 64%. Promoted on that evidence, and recorded as an override — moving a bar after seeing results is exactly what pre-registration exists to prevent, so it is reported rather than hidden.
1111209-13

Guidance re-tune, cfg 1.5 → 2.5 Adopted

The deployed guidance scale had been validated when both stages were different models. A sweep found it mis-tuned for the current pipeline.

Accuracy rose across the board — 2/3/4-speaker 84.3/78.3/74.9 → 85.7/80.0/77.0, corpus-wide 85.2% → 86.2%, low-SNR 78.7% → 80.3% — for −0.09 dB SI-SDR and +0.5 pp catastrophic.

Reason for adoptionThe single largest recovery in the campaign, at zero training cost. Accuracy plateaued at 2.5 while outliers began rising at 3.0, so the sweep was stopped there rather than pushed further.
1116409-14

Log-gain pair, both stages co-adapted Rejected

The obvious follow-up to entry I: if the frozen Stage 2 was the problem, adapt it to the log-gain Stage 1. Failed every bar — corpus-wide 83.5% against a required 86.5%.

The mechanism is the finding: Stage 2's improvement over Stage 1 collapsed from +74.1% mel to +11.7%, and went negative at low SNR. The final output was worse in absolute terms despite Stage 1's error being 2.6× lower.

What it establishedPipeline quality is not a function of Stage 1 accuracy alone. 300k steps of Stage 2 training against multiplicative-mask output cannot be re-pointed at a different input family in 25k, and the residual Stage 2 learned to predict is much smaller when its input is already near the target. Recast entry I's failure as structural, not mere distribution shift.
1118409-15

Onset fine-tune Rejected

Targeted at the onset gate found by ear (§9.4). Improved it — 7/24 gated → 4/24, 2-speaker fully clean — but the bar was ≤1/24, and 4-speaker did not move at all.

Reason for rejectionMissed its pre-registered bar. In hindsight the shortfall is explained by the ablation below: it adjusted Stage 2 alone for a defect that lives in the coupling between stages.
1120209-16

Inference-side onset repair Rejected

A silent lead-in spliced over the first second, so the weak region falls in frames discarded before vocoding — no retraining required. Implemented opt-in so the disabled path stays bit-identical.

Gate 7/24 → 6/24 (one sample), accuracy unchanged to slightly down, 4-speaker again unmoved.

Reason for rejectionTwo independent mitigations — one retraining, one inference-side — failed in the same condition, which is what the interaction diagnosis predicts.
1121009-17

CFG warm-up Rejected

Flagged twice in the project record as the only change that had improved every measured metric with nothing worse (mel −11%, SI-SDR +0.48 dB). It had never been measured on accuracy, because only one of five evaluation scripts exposed the flag.

Wired through all five and measured: the quality gains replicated at full corpus scale, but accuracy fell in every condition — 4-speaker gave back 2.4 pp, the entire gain the guidance re-tune had bought.

Reason for rejectionWarm-up runs the first two of four steps unguided, so it is simply weaker guidance re-parameterized — time-averaged ≈ cfg 1.75, on an axis already swept and deliberately optimized in the other direction. It reproduced cfg 2.0's accuracy almost exactly. "No metric worse" held only because the diagnostic that surfaced it never measured accuracy.
1121609-18

Longer enrollment, 3.0 s → 5.0 s Rejected

Enrollment length is a strong known lever in TSE and had never been varied — the config key did not exist, so every result in the project had used the hardcoded 3.0 s default.

Worse across the board. The diagnostic detail: the ceiling itself fell, 90.3% → 89.6%, and the mixture probe moved too.

Reason for rejectionThose probes depend only on the enrollment embedding, so the loss was not in extraction — a longer window produced a worse speaker reference, and everything downstream followed. This rejects "a longer window on one utterance", not "more enrollment audio"; pooling several utterances remains untested.
1121809-18

Heun ODE solver Rejected

Only Euler had ever been used. A 2nd-order solver costs two velocity evaluations per step, so Heun at 2 steps was compared against Euler at 4 — equal compute, the only fair comparison.

Worse nearly everywhere, worst at low SNR (−2.6 pp).

Reason for rejectionStage 2 is trained with a rectified flow objective, which optimizes for straight trajectories — and Euler is exact on a straight line. There was no integration error left to recover, while halving the steps halved the number of CFG applications, which this pipeline is demonstrably sensitive to. A unit test confirms the solver is more accurate on a curved field; the model simply does not produce one.
1122009-18

Alternate speaker projection Rejected

Two projection checkpoints existed; every script defaulted to one and the other had never been evaluated. Headline accuracy came back identical at 86.2%.

Reason for rejectionAUC fell (0.9361 → 0.9308), and the probes gave it away: the mixture baseline rose while the ceiling fell, narrowing the spread between doing nothing and ground truth from 19.8 to 17.9 pp. It is a less discriminative embedding space measuring on a squashed scale, not a better system.

08Ceiling analysis

Late in Phase One the question became: how much room is actually left? The oracle decomposition and the ceiling probe answer it quantitatively.

Headroom at the trained operating point, 2 speakers
ConfigurationAccuracyInterpretation
Current system85.7%deployed
With a perfect Stage 190.0%oracle energy deletion
Ceiling — clean target audio90.3%vocoder + speaker encoder limited
Theoretical100%—

The ceiling probe passes the ground-truth clean target through the same mel → HiFi-GAN → WavLM chain as every other probe. So 90.3% is what perfect extraction of perfectly clean speech scores. The remaining 9.7 pp is lost by the vocoder and speaker encoder before extraction quality is involved at all.

That splits the remaining headroom into two unequal pools:

  • Extraction: ~4.6 pp, and the oracle shows a perfect Stage 1 captures 4.3 of it. Stage 2 and the operating point are effectively saturated at the trained SNR.
  • Measurement chain: ~9.7 pp — more than twice what remains in the extractor. Improving it would raise the ceiling and the system number together, which is a legitimate finding to report but not the same claim as a better extractor.

This prediction was then tested and held. Five consecutive inference-side experiments — onset padding, CFG warm-up, longer enrollment, Heun, alternate projection — all failed to move the system. That is not bad luck; it is what a saturated operating point looks like. The analysis directed Phase Two away from inference-time tuning and toward Stage 1 and the measurement chain.

09Characterized limitations

Four, each with an established root cause. Two were substantially mitigated, one closed, one remains open.

9.1  t≈0 training-coverage gap Mitigated 3.6×

At t=0 the flow input equals Stage 1's output exactly, giving the velocity field no interpolation signal and forcing it to extrapolate the whole trajectory from one point. Guidance amplifies the resulting disagreement into an oversized first step. Diagnosed by measuring ‖v_cond − v_uncond‖ per step, then fixed by over-weighting t≈0 during fine-tuning: catastrophic rate 12.7% → 3.5%. Not solved — the worst samples still collapse.

9.2  SNR coverage below 1 dB Closed

The mixer's snr_min = 1.0 floor meant the model had never trained on a mixture where the target is quieter than the interferer — 60.6% of Libri2Mix. In that regime the system was actively harmful: 61.3% accuracy against a 64.0% do-nothing baseline. Closed by the Stage 1 fine-tune plus Stage 2 adaptation plus guidance re-tune: 80.3%, SI-SDR −15.86 → +6.51 dB.

9.3  Speaker-count generalization Mitigated

Trained only on 2-speaker mixtures; 3 and 4 speakers were out of distribution. The speaker-count curriculum lifted 4-speaker from 72.6% to 77.0%, where it remains.

9.4  Onset gate Open

Found by listening, not by any metric. The first ~0.5 s of affected utterances sits on a flat ≈−60 dB floor and then switches on abruptly — a gate, not gradual damping. Interior onsets after silence are reproduced perfectly (+0.29 dB), so it is a position-0 effect: frame 0 is the only frame with no left context for the DiT's rotary attention, and Stage 2 generates its output where Stage 1 merely scales the mixture.

Neither promotion causes it alone — each is indistinguishable from the pre-campaign pipeline. Only the pair gates, and superadditively: summing the individual effects predicts ≈2/24 and −2.96 dB against a measured 7/24 and −4.67 dB. Every gated 4-speaker sample requires both new checkpoints present.

This removes the cheapest remedy — there is no single promotion to revert — and converges with entry N from the opposite direction. Both say the same thing: in this two-stage design the stages are co-adapted, and neither stage's quality predicts the pipeline's. Two mitigations were measured against a bar fixed in advance and both failed, both in the 4-speaker condition. Reported as a characterized limitation with an established cause.

Why a metric never caught it. The speaker embedding is mean-pooled over the whole utterance, so half a second of a four-to-ten-second clip barely shifts it. The battery was structurally incapable of seeing this defect — which is the argument for keeping a listening test in the loop regardless of how comprehensive the quantitative suite looks.

10Engineering record

Defects found and fixed during Phase One. Several would have silently corrupted results rather than produced errors, which is the reason they are worth reporting.

Defects, and what each would have cost
DefectEffect if unfixed
Padding dilution — enrollment clips shorter than 3.0 s were zero-padded before mean-pooled embedding Diluted every speaker vector. Fixing it moved 2/3/4-speaker accuracy +0.6/+0.2/+0.3 pp — a measurement correction, not a model change, and it re-based every subsequent comparison.
Unseeded speaker projection Non-reproducible conditioning between runs.
Upstream LibriMix bug — write_noise() called unconditionally even for mix_clean, writing into a directory never created Every generation worker crashed. Patched in the vendored copy with a dated comment so it is not mistaken for upstream behaviour.
Metadata path level — LibriMix writes metadata as a sibling of the split directory, not inside it Three eval jobs failed at the last step after full GPU allocation and checkpoint loading.

RIR contamination — OpenSLR resource 28 ships 843 noise recordings alongside the impulse responses The loader globs recursively, so ~2% of the reverb pool would have been noise clips convolved as impulse responses. Caught before any training used it; the config now points at the RIR-only subtree.
Silent download failure — the downloader printed one error line and continued A 13 MB log ended in "Download complete!" with a split missing. Now collects failures and exits non-zero.
Dead code that always crashed — summing human-readable size strings The downloader's main path had never successfully run; the data on disk had been fetched another way.
Partial-extraction trap — every existence check treated a split directory as complete The sharpest one. An interrupted extraction left 83,232 of 104,014 files — 80% complete. Because tar extracts sequentially by path, the missing fifth was whole speaker directories, which for a speaker-conditioned task is far worse than scattered losses. Now verified by file count against a per-split table.

A recurring lesson

Three separate times, a change that improved every metric under observation turned out not to be free: the mel/SI-SDR divergence at low SNR, the log-gain result where a 2.6× better Stage 1 produced a worse pipeline, and CFG warm-up, which improved every quality metric while costing accuracy on an axis nobody had measured.

A change that improves every metric you happen to be looking at has not been shown to be free. The useful habit is to ask which axis the current diagnostic cannot see, and measure that one before believing the result.

11Infrastructure

A material fraction of Phase One's elapsed time went to cluster faults rather than experiments. Recorded because they shaped the schedule and because the diagnostic habits they forced are reusable.

Infrastructure failures encountered
FaultDiagnosis and response
SLURM auth daemon died mid-jobmunge.socket.2 vanished during extraction, killing the job. Cluster-level; the job scripts were made idempotent so a resubmit resumes rather than restarts.
GPU occupied by a foreign process14,994 MiB of a 16,384 MiB P100 held by another process. Per-sample time degraded from 5.5 s to 76.8 s — a 14× slowdown — and a 5-minute stage took 347 minutes. Visible only because the launcher prints nvidia-smi at job start.

Filesystem fullA 15.5-hour training run died at step 21,874 with ENOSPC. The shared volume also swung 161 GB → 38 GB overnight through other users' activity.
NFS write failuresExtraction of a checksum-verified archive failed twice with Input/output error and partial writes, with 450 GB+ free. A storage-layer fault, not capacity — unresolved and referred onward.

Two practices came out of this and are now standard in every job script: print the environment diagnostics before doing work (GPU state, disk, resolved interpreter), because the GPU contention was invisible without it; and make every long job idempotent and verify completeness by count, not existence, because three separate failures left partially-complete artifacts that downstream checks would have accepted.

Phase One ends with the extraction side saturated at its trained operating point and the remaining headroom precisely located. Phase Two follows that analysis rather than continuing to tune inference.
